\section{Mechanism Design}

\subsection{Initiatives, not Proposals}
\signals{} works with \emph{Initiatives}---ideas or actions seeking support. Initiatives live inside Boards (configuration sets), and DAOs can run multiple Boards with different parameters. Board configs are on-chain, so DAOs can govern them directly. A factory deploys new boards as minimal proxies of a reference implementation and calls \texttt{initialize} with the full configuration; successful deployments emit \texttt{BoardCreated}.

\subsection{Board Configuration}
A board is initialized with immutable parameters:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Underlying Token}: ERC-20 address used for locks (and used for eligibility checks).
  \item \textbf{Lock Interval}: Time granularity for lock and decay calculations (e.g., 1 day).
  \item \textbf{Max Lock Intervals}: Upper bound on lock duration (expressed in intervals).
  \item \textbf{Decay Curve}: \texttt{linear} or \texttt{exponential}, with parameters.
  \item \textbf{Acceptance Criteria}: \texttt{anyoneCanAccept}, \texttt{ownerMustFollowThreshold}, fixed threshold, and optional percentage-of-supply threshold. The effective threshold is \(\max(\)fixed, percentage \(\times\) totalSupply\()\).
  \item \textbf{Eligibility}: Proposer and supporter requirements: \texttt{None}, \texttt{MinBalance}, or \texttt{MinBalanceAndDuration} (uses ERC20Votes checkpoints). Optional \texttt{minLockAmount}.
  \item \textbf{Inactivity Timeout}: Time after last activity when an initiative can be expired by the owner.
  \item \textbf{Release Timelock}: Optional delay before early redemption after acceptance.
  \item \textbf{Board Open/Closed}: \texttt{boardOpenAt} and \texttt{boardClosedAt} gate propose/support. Owner can also \emph{close} or \emph{cancel} a board (cancellation unlocks all locks for redemption).
\end{itemize}
Metadata for the board (and for initiatives) includes a title, body, and up to five attachments with non-empty URIs.

\subsection{Token-Locking and ERC-721 Positions}
Participants lock the board's ERC-20 for a chosen duration (in intervals) to express commitment. Each lock mints a transferable ERC-721 position (one token per lock). The token name derives from the underlying ERC-20 name and the symbol is prefixed with \texttt{sx}. Positions are burned on redemption when the underlying is returned. A zero-duration lock carries nominal weight and is redeemable immediately; longer durations increase initial weight but are subject to decay.

\subsection{Lifecycle}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Propose}: \texttt{proposeInitiative} or \texttt{proposeInitiativeWithLock}. The latter both proposes and adds initial support. Propose/support require the board to be open and the caller to meet proposer/supporter requirements.
  \item \textbf{Support}: \texttt{supportInitiative} transfers underlying into the board, mints an ERC-721 lock, and updates initiative activity. Support can only be added while the initiative is \texttt{Proposed}.
  \item \textbf{Accept}: \texttt{acceptInitiative} moves \texttt{Proposed} \(\rightarrow\) \texttt{Accepted}. If \texttt{anyoneCanAccept=false}, only the owner can call. If \texttt{ownerMustFollowThreshold=true} or a non-owner calls (when allowed), the initiative must meet the acceptance threshold at call time (sum of decayed weights).
  \item \textbf{Expire}: \texttt{expireInitiative} moves \texttt{Proposed} \(\rightarrow\) \texttt{Expired} after the inactivity timeout; callable by the owner only.
  \item \textbf{Redeem}: \texttt{redeemLock} or \texttt{redeemLocksForInitiative}. A lock is redeemable if: the board is cancelled, the initiative is expired, the lock's own duration has elapsed, or the initiative is accepted and the release timelock (if any) has passed. Redemption burns the lock NFT and transfers the underlying back. If incentives are configured, reward claims are processed atomically during redemption.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Calculating Support}
Time is measured in discrete intervals (the board's lock interval). Let a lock be created at time \(t_0\) with amount \(T\) and duration \(D\) (in intervals). At time \(t\), the number of elapsed intervals is \(\lfloor (t - t_0)/\text{interval}\rfloor\). Weight contributions follow the chosen decay curve and are bounded below by the nominal amount:
\[
  \text{Weight}(t) = \max\big(\text{Curve}(T, D, \text{elapsed}),\ T\big).
\]
For linear decay with rate \(k \in [0, 1]\) (WAD precision),
\[
  \text{Curve}_\text{linear}(T, D, i) = T \cdot D - \frac{T \cdot i \cdot k}{1e18}.
\]
For exponential decay with multiplier \(m \in [0, 1]\) (WAD),
\[
  \text{Curve}_\text{exp}(T, D, i) = \big(T \cdot D\big) \cdot m^i.
\]
Support for initiative \(i\) is the sum over active, non-withdrawn locks:
\[
  \text{Support}_i(t) = \sum_{\ell \in \text{ActiveLocks}_i} \text{Weight}_\ell(t).
\]
This design preserves a guarantee: even at maximum decay, a supporter’s weight never drops below their nominal lock amount \(T\).

\subsection{Acceptance and Thresholds}
Boards define acceptance criteria with two levers:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Access}: \texttt{anyoneCanAccept} gates whether non-owners can accept. Owners may optionally bypass thresholds when \texttt{ownerMustFollowThreshold=false}.
  \item \textbf{Thresholds}: a fixed threshold and an optional percentage-of-total-supply threshold; the effective threshold is the maximum of the two at decision time.
\end{itemize}
Once accepted, an initiative unlocks after the board-level release timelock (default \(0\)), incentivizing supporters to back initiatives expected to succeed.

\subsection{Inactivity and Expiration}
Each initiative tracks \emph{last activity}. If the initiative remains \texttt{Proposed} and no support is added for more than the board's inactivity timeout, the owner can expire it. Expired initiatives stop accepting support and become redeemable immediately.

\subsection{Optional Endorsements}
If a DAO uses delegated voting, delegates may not own the tokens they wield. Boards could optionally require binary \emph{Endorsements} tied to delegated voting power in addition to lockups. Caps on endorsements per Initiative encourage delegates to act early and visibly. This mechanism is not implemented in the current contracts and is left as a module for future work.

\subsection{Factory Deployment}
Boards are deployed via a minimal-proxy factory (EIP-1167 clones). The factory validates basic metadata (non-empty title, attachment limits) and emits \texttt{BoardCreated(owner, metadata)} upon success. This keeps per-board deployment gas low while preserving upgrade-independent logic in the reference implementation.
