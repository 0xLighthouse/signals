\section{Paid Incentives in Signals}
\subsection{Motivation}
DAOs often face low participation: most holders do not see sufficient upside to engage, and reputational risk pushes signaling to the last minute. Some DAOs spend \$1M+ per year on delegate programs. Signals inherits similar participation risks, so we add configurable incentives that reward early, accurate support and penalize weak or misaligned signaling.

\subsection{Mechanism 1: General Participation Incentives}
A Board can be funded (e.g., from treasury) to share rewards with supporters of any Initiative that gets actioned. Reward share can be time-weighted so early supporters earn more:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Improves effort-reward ratio to motivate broader participation.
  \item Competes for early signaling instead of last-minute pile-on.
  \item Supporting an unpopular Initiative risks missing rewards elsewhere, encouraging intentional choices.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mechanism 2: Sponsored Initiative Incentives}
Anyone can add rewards to a specific Initiative. If it expires, rewards revert to the sponsor; if actioned, rewards are split among supporters, treasury, and protocol according to a configured ratio (e.g., 80/15/5). Benefits:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Sponsors signal conviction by staking funds on success.
  \item External partners can prove commitment to a DAO initiative up front.
  \item Popular initiatives can crowd-source additional rewards to amplify sentiment.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Impact}
Rewards amplify Signals’ core design: only positive support, opportunity cost via locks, and time-weighted influence that lets smaller holders compete. Incentives raise the cost of frivolous signaling and make early, accurate support economically meaningful, improving resilience against brigading or apathy-driven noise.
