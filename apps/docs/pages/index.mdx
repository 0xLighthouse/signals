# Signals Protocol

Signals is a conviction-weighted governance primitive. Community members propose initiatives and lock governance tokens behind the ones they want prioritised. Locking tokens for longer generates more support weight, at the cost of having those tokens unavailable for the duration. When token supply is finite and lock durations are real, participants are forced to choose between competing initiatives. That constraint is the mechanism's core property.

## Lock mechanics

Each lock's support weight starts at `amount × duration` and decreases over time according to a configured decay curve (linear or exponential). Stale support loses influence automatically — an initiative that was popular three months ago but has attracted no new backing will fall below threshold without any intervention. Sustained conviction requires sustained participation.

## Thresholds and acceptance

An initiative is eligible for acceptance when its aggregate support weight crosses a configured threshold. The threshold is the greater of:

- A percentage of total token supply, which prevents a single large holder from pushing an initiative through alone (whale capture).
- A fixed minimum, which prevents spam initiatives from reaching acceptance with trivial token amounts (dust attacks).

Acceptance permissions are configurable: owner-only, permissionless, or owner with threshold bypass.

On acceptance, locked tokens enter a release timelock (configurable, can be zero), after which supporters reclaim their tokens. Initiatives that never reach threshold can be expired by the board owner after an inactivity timeout — a configurable period with no new locks — which returns tokens immediately.

## Transferable lock positions

Each lock mints an ERC-721 token representing the position. Lock NFTs are fully transferable, which means support positions can be traded on secondary markets.

If a lock position on a popular initiative trades above the value of the underlying tokens, the premium reflects the market's estimate of that initiative's likelihood of acceptance and the value of associated rewards. The spread between lock price and underlying token value is a price signal for conviction.

## Incentives

An optional incentives pool distributes participation rewards weighted by lock timing. The reward pool divides the initiative's lifetime into 24 equal time buckets, and locks placed in earlier buckets receive proportionally higher shares. Earlier supporters earn more, which rewards conviction over bandwagoning.

Separate bounties can also be attached to individual initiatives. On acceptance, bounty payouts split between supporters, the protocol, and a treasury at configured ratios.

## Game theory

The mechanism's expected behaviours follow from opportunity cost, not explicit rules:

- Participants support initiatives they genuinely believe in, because locking tokens has real opportunity cost.
- Participants avoid supporting initiatives they are indifferent to, because those tokens could back something with a better chance of acceptance.
- Participants may support initiatives they are personally indifferent to but believe the broader community will prioritise, because accepted initiatives return tokens and optionally distribute rewards.

## Initiative lifecycle

An initiative moves through four states:

- **Proposed** — accepting locks.
- **Accepted** — threshold met, tokens timelocked for release.
- **Expired** — inactive beyond the configured timeout, tokens returned.
- **Cancelled** — board-level emergency, tokens returned immediately.

Every terminal state returns tokens to holders. Participants never lose principal through the mechanism itself.

## Board administration

The board owner can:

- **Shut down** a board — stops new activity; existing locks follow normal redemption rules.
- **Cancel** a board — releases all locked tokens at once, regardless of state.

Cancellation exists because governance systems need a clean unwind path when assumptions break.
