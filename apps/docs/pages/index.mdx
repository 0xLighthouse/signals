# Signals Protocol

### Token holders reveal collective priorities by locking governance tokens behind the initiatives they support

Signals is a conviction-weighted governance primitive. Community members propose **initiatives**, and other participants lock their existing governance tokens to back the ones they believe matter. Locking tokens for longer generates more **support weight**, at the cost of having those tokens unavailable for the duration. When token supply is finite and lock durations are real, participants are forced to choose between competing initiatives. That constraint is the mechanism's core property: support that costs nothing reveals nothing.

### Support weight decays, which keeps priorities current

Each lock's weight starts at amount multiplied by duration, then decreases over time according to a configured **decay curve** (linear or exponential). This means stale support loses influence. An initiative that was popular three months ago but has attracted no new backing will naturally fall below threshold. Sustained conviction requires sustained participation.

### Acceptance is threshold-based, not vote-based

When an initiative's aggregate support weight crosses a configured threshold, it can be **accepted**. The threshold is the greater of a percentage of total token supply or a fixed minimum, preventing both whale capture and dust attacks. Acceptance permissions are configurable: owner-only, permissionless, or owner with threshold bypass.

On acceptance, locked tokens enter a **release timelock** (configurable, can be zero), after which supporters reclaim their tokens. Initiatives that never reach threshold can be **expired** by the board owner after an inactivity timeout, which returns tokens immediately.

### The incentive structure penalizes indiscriminate support

The game theory rests on three expected behaviors:

- Participants support initiatives they genuinely believe in, because locking tokens has real opportunity cost.
- Participants avoid supporting initiatives they're indifferent to, because those tokens could be backing something with a better chance of acceptance.
- Participants may support initiatives they're personally indifferent to but believe the broader community will prioritize, because accepted initiatives return tokens (and optionally distribute rewards).

### Lock positions are transferable NFTs

Each lock mints an **ERC721 token** representing the position. Lock NFTs are fully transferable, which means support positions can be traded on secondary markets. This is a deliberate design choice: it creates a price signal for conviction.

### Rewards attach to successful initiatives

An optional **incentives pool** distributes participation rewards weighted by lock timing. Earlier supporters receive higher multipliers through a 24-bucket time-weighted system. Separate **bounties** can be attached to specific initiatives, splitting payouts between supporters, the protocol, and a treasury on acceptance.

### Board closure is an emergency release valve

The board owner can **close** a board (stops new activity, existing locks follow normal redemption rules) or **cancel** it (immediately releases all locked tokens regardless of state). Cancellation exists because governance systems need a way to unwind cleanly when assumptions break.

### Initiative lifecycle

An initiative moves through four possible states: **Proposed** (accepting locks), **Accepted** (threshold met, tokens timelocked for release), **Expired** (inactive too long, tokens returned), or **Cancelled** (board-level emergency, tokens returned immediately).
